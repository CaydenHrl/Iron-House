How to price online personal training in 2025

By Iron House · 7 min read

Most trainers charge too little. Not because the market won't pay more — but because they don't know what the market actually pays. Here's the full picture.

Pricing is one of the hardest parts of building an online personal training business. Charge too little and you burn out taking on too many clients to hit a livable income. Charge too much before you've built trust and you scare away leads. Charge the wrong amount for the wrong audience and your retention craters.

The goal isn't to find the lowest price that gets you clients. The goal is to find the right price for the right clients — and understand how to get there as your business grows.

$197
The median monthly rate for online personal training in 2025, across all experience levels and service tiers. Most new trainers charge well below this — unnecessarily.

What the market actually looks like

Online personal training prices vary widely based on what's included, who the trainer is, and who the client is. Here's a realistic picture of the market right now:

TierMonthly RateWhat's typically included
Entry-level / new coaches$75–$149Basic programming, limited check-ins
Mid-range (most coaches)$149–$299Custom programming, weekly check-ins, form feedback
Premium / specialized$299–$500+Daily availability, nutrition coaching, niche expertise
High-end / 1:1 intensive$500–$1,200+Near-daily communication, VIP access, transformation programs

If you're brand new to online coaching, starting in the $99–$149 range while you build testimonials and systems is reasonable. But most trainers stay there long after they've earned the right to charge more. The jump from $99 to $199 is psychological for trainers — clients barely notice it.

The factors that actually justify higher pricing

Specialization

A trainer who works with anyone charges generalist rates. A trainer who works specifically with postpartum women, competitive powerlifters, or busy executives over 40 can charge 30–50% more for the same amount of work — because they've reduced the perceived risk for the client. Specialization signals expertise. Expertise justifies premium pricing.

What's included

Clients don't buy training. They buy outcomes. If your offer includes custom programming + weekly video check-ins + nutrition guidance + unlimited messaging, that's worth $249/month. If it's just "a program sent monthly," that's worth $99. Be specific about what's included and price the full value of it.

Your track record

Before and after results, client testimonials, and visible transformations are the clearest signal that working with you produces outcomes. The more of this social proof you have, the more you can charge. Trainers who document client results consistently earn the ability to raise rates 6–12 months faster than those who don't.

Accessibility and responsiveness

High-end clients pay for access. If you respond within hours and make clients feel genuinely prioritized, that's a premium service. Price it like one. Unlimited messaging and same-day responses command a different rate than monthly email check-ins.

"The moment I stopped thinking of my price as 'what someone would pay' and started thinking of it as 'what this is actually worth,' my rates went up and my client satisfaction went up with them."

How to raise your rates without losing clients

Grandfather your current clients

When you raise rates, give existing clients a heads-up and honor their current rate for 60–90 days. Most will appreciate the notice and stay. New clients onboard at the higher rate immediately.

Add value before raising price

Before you increase your rate, add something to your offer — a new check-in format, nutrition templates, or a progress review call. You're not just charging more; you're delivering more. Frame it that way.

Raise rates on new clients only

The lowest-risk approach: keep current clients where they are and set a higher price for every new client going forward. Within 6 months, a significant portion of your roster will be at the new rate naturally.

The underpricing trap — and why it costs you clients

Here's the counterintuitive truth about pricing: underpricing doesn't just hurt your income. It hurts client results.

When someone pays $75 a month for training, they treat it like a $75/month subscription. Easy to pause, easy to cancel, easy to deprioritize. When someone pays $249 a month, they show up. They do the work. Skin in the game produces results — and results produce referrals.

Your pricing is also a signal to the kind of client you attract. Budget pricing attracts clients who are shopping for the cheapest option and will leave the moment something cheaper appears. Premium pricing attracts clients who are serious about their results and willing to invest in getting them.

What to charge when you're just starting out

  • Take 1–3 clients at a discounted rate ($79–$99/month) explicitly in exchange for detailed testimonials and transformation photos. These are your proof-of-concept clients.
  • Price your next batch at $149/month. You now have social proof. You've earned it.
  • After 10 clients and 3 months of results, evaluate moving to $199–$249. By then your systems are tighter, your programming is better, and your time is more valuable.

The worst thing a new trainer can do is stay at $75/month for two years because it feels "safer." You cap your income, devalue your work, and attract clients who don't produce results you can show off.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I charge for online personal training as a beginner?

As a new online coach, $99–$149/month is a reasonable starting range if you have a certification and a clear offer. Taking 1–3 clients at a lower rate in exchange for testimonials first is smart — but get out of that range as soon as you have proof of results. Don't stay there for longer than 3 months.

Should I charge per session or per month for online training?

Monthly retainer pricing almost always wins for online training. Per-session pricing creates uncertainty for both parties and makes it easy for clients to skip. A monthly subscription creates commitment, consistency, and better outcomes — which is what you're actually selling.

How do I charge more without scaring off clients?

Add value, get specific about what's included, and collect and display results. Clients don't resist paying $249 for a coach who clearly delivers — they resist paying $249 when they're not sure it's worth it. The answer to price resistance is almost always better positioning and more social proof, not a lower price.

What's a good annual income goal for an online personal trainer?

At $199/month with 15 clients, you're at roughly $36,000/year. At $249/month with 20 clients, you're approaching $60,000/year. Most full-time online coaches target 15–25 clients as a sustainable roster. Beyond 25 clients, service quality typically suffers without additional systems or team support.
